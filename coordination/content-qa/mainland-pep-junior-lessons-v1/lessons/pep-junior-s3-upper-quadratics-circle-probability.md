## 简体中文教材

### 引入
初三上学期的重点是把“求解、看图、证明、估计可能性”放进同一套推理习惯。

### 学习目标
1. 选择合适方法解一元二次方程。
2. 用顶点、对称轴和开口解释二次函数图象。
3. 用清晰样本空间计算简单概率，并识别圆中的基本关系。

### 预备热身
因式分解 \(x^2-5x+6\)，并写出它等于 \(0\) 时的两个解。

### 核心讲解
一元二次方程可以用因式分解、配方法或公式法求解。二次函数图象是抛物线，顶点和对称轴能帮助判断最大值、最小值和变化趋势。圆中很多结论必须依赖半径、弦、圆心角等明确关系。概率计算要先列出不重不漏的样本空间。

### 例题精讲
例1 例1：二次方程
解方程 \(x^2-7x+10=0\)。
因式分解得 \((x-5)(x-2)=0\)，所以 \(x=5\) 或 \(x=2\)。
检查: 代回两个解都能使左边为 \(0\)。

例2 例2：概率样本空间
从数字 \(1,2,3,4\) 中随机取一个数，取到偶数的概率是多少？
样本空间有 \(4\) 个结果。偶数为 \(2\) 和 \(4\)，有 \(2\) 个有利结果，所以概率为 \(\frac{2}{4}=\frac12\)。
检查: 有利结果不能重复，样本空间不能遗漏。

### 常见错误
1. 只看判别式符号，不解释解的意义。
2. 把顶点和对称轴混淆。
3. 圆中凭图猜相等关系。
4. 概率中样本空间列不完整。

### 误区诊断
1. 若学生把 \(x=3\) 当作抛物线顶点，请追问顶点应是点 \((x,y)\) 还是直线。
2. 若概率结果大于一，请让学生重新数总结果和有利结果。

### 策略清单
1. 二次方程先看能否因式分解。
2. 二次函数先找对称轴和顶点。
3. 圆题先标半径、弦和圆心。
4. 概率题先列样本空间。

### 小练习
小练习 1: 解 \(x^2-9=0\)。
答案: x=3 or x=-3. \((x-3)(x+3)=0\)，所以 \(x=3\) 或 \(x=-3\)。
小练习 2: 函数 \(y=(x-1)^2+4\) 的顶点是什么？
答案: (1,4). 顶点式直接给出顶点 \((1,4)\)。
小练习 3: 掷一枚均匀硬币一次，出现正面的概率是多少？
答案: 1/2. 样本空间为 \(2\) 个结果，正面为 \(1\) 个，所以概率为 \(\frac12\)。

### 考试策略
综合题先判断属于代数、图象、几何还是概率层，再把每层的条件写清楚。

### 拓展任务
用同一个二次函数设计一道求根题和一道图象解释题，并说明二者如何关联。

### 出门票
概率计算中，为什么必须先确定样本空间？

### 术语表
一元二次方程: 只含一个未知数且最高次数为二的方程。
二次函数: 可写成二次多项式形式的函数。
圆: 到定点距离相等的所有点组成的图形。
样本空间: 随机试验所有可能结果的集合。

## English Lesson Textbook

### Hook
The main S3 upper habit is to connect solving, graph reading, proof, and probability reasoning in one reasoning system.

### Objectives
1. Choose suitable methods to solve quadratic equations.
2. Use vertex, axis of symmetry, and opening direction to interpret quadratic graphs.
3. Calculate simple probability using a clear sample space and identify basic circle relationships.

### Prerequisite Warm-Up
Factor \(x^2-5x+6\) and write the two solutions when it equals \(0\).

### Core Concept
Quadratic equations can be solved by factorization, completing the square, or the quadratic formula. A quadratic graph is a parabola; its vertex and axis help identify maximum, minimum, and trend. Circle conclusions must rely on clear relationships involving radius, chord, central angle, and related objects. Probability calculation begins with a complete, non-overlapping sample space.

### Worked Examples
Example 1 Example 1: Quadratic Equation
Solve \(x^2-7x+10=0\).
Factor to get \((x-5)(x-2)=0\), so \(x=5\) or \(x=2\).
Check: Substituting either solution makes the left side equal \(0\).

Example 2 Example 2: Probability Sample Space
Choose one number at random from \(1,2,3,4\). What is the probability of choosing an even number?
The sample space has \(4\) outcomes. The even numbers are \(2\) and \(4\), giving \(2\) favorable outcomes, so the probability is \(\frac{2}{4}=\frac12\).
Check: Favorable outcomes must not repeat, and the sample space must not omit outcomes.

### Common Pitfalls
1. Looking only at the discriminant sign without explaining solution meaning.
2. Confusing vertex with axis of symmetry.
3. Guessing equality in circle geometry from the drawing.
4. Listing an incomplete probability sample space.

### Misconception Clinic
1. If a learner calls \(x=3\) the vertex, ask whether a vertex is a point \((x,y)\) or a line.
2. If a probability is greater than one, ask learners to recount total and favorable outcomes.

### Strategy Checklist
1. For quadratic equations, first check whether factorization works.
2. For quadratic functions, find the axis and vertex first.
3. For circle problems, mark radii, chords, and center first.
4. For probability, list the sample space first.

### Checkpoints
Checkpoint 1: Solve \(x^2-9=0\).
Answer: x=3 or x=-3. \((x-3)(x+3)=0\), so \(x=3\) or \(x=-3\).
Checkpoint 2: What is the vertex of \(y=(x-1)^2+4\)?
Answer: (1,4). Vertex form gives the vertex \((1,4)\).
Checkpoint 3: Flip a fair coin once. What is the probability of heads?
Answer: 1/2. The sample space has \(2\) outcomes and heads is \(1\) outcome, so the probability is \(\frac12\).

### Exam-Style Strategy
For integrated tasks, identify whether each part is algebraic, graphical, geometric, or probabilistic, then state the conditions for that layer clearly.

### Extension
Use one quadratic function to create one root-finding task and one graph-interpretation task, then explain how they are connected.

### Exit Ticket
In probability, why must the sample space be identified first?

### Glossary
quadratic equation: An equation in one unknown whose highest power is two.
quadratic function: A function that can be written as a quadratic polynomial.
circle: The set of points at a fixed distance from a fixed point.
sample space: The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment.

## What this means for the remediation plan

Plan item 10 ("de-duplicate: pep-primary 49%, bnu-high 85.6%, hjb-high 204 groups, drop pep-high's two legacy generations") is still three-quarters live and **should not be de-prioritised on the strength of the corpus shrinking**. pep-primary's 49%, bnu-high's 85.6% and pep-high's 580/8.3% are numerically untouched; only the hjb-high clause is moot, and it is moot only for as long as the de-reach holds — the pack is still in `data/`, still bundled, still tested, and its "remediated" v3/v4 successors are *more* monotonous (37.5× skeleton reuse) than the v2 they were meant to replace, so promoting them would make this finding worse, not better. Two things did change materially. First, the shipped duplication rate went **up** (7.0% → 8.5%): the withdrawal removed 4,500 near-pristine items (bnu-primary and hjb-primary, both 1.0× skeleton reuse, 0% internal duplication) and left the padding behind, so China's remaining bank is now measurably more repetitive than the one the audit condemned — anyone quoting "17,700 → 11,700, problem shrinking" has it backwards. Second, the "others <1%" row should be struck from the dedup workstream entirely: bnu-junior and hjb-junior have **zero** internal duplication, and their entire contribution to G4 was five bnu↔hjb shared items, which is G6's cheap-and-cosmetic problem, not a padding problem. Re-scope item 10 to exactly three targets — pep-primary (rebuild the draft pool), pep-high (drop seed-v1 + rag-v2, *then* separately fix F11, because dropping the legacy generations leaves 68.5% mathematical repetition untouched and the freshly-measured 63.2× post-boilerplate skeleton reuse standing), and bnu-high (unfixable item-by-item at 85.6%; item 13's "re-author" is the right call) — and add the pep-high skeleton number to the case, since it is the worst monotony figure in the shipped bank and the original report never measured it.
